Your browser is repeating the experiment from {{ with $bm }}{{ $ref }}{{ else }}{{ $ref }}{{ end }} right now. It reads the same 16 MiB of memory twice: once in order, and once jumping 8 KiB at a time. Jumping defeats the CPU cache, so it is slower. The question is by how much, on whatever you are reading this on.

Starts once the page has finished loading.

Method: a 2048 × 2048 int32 matrix summed along rows, then down columns, in a background worker. Three untimed warm-ups, then 15 interleaved samples of 4 passes; median reported. cv is the spread between samples: above 5% the run can't be trusted, and a browser can't pin itself to one core. For reference, {{ $ref }} measured jumping 5.4× slower on a GB10 performance core and 18× slower on an efficiency core.
